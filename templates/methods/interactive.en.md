**What it solves**: With one-click story generation, the author has no way in and the reader can only watch. When the model chooses the direction itself, the story tends to go flat or off course, and over longer lengths it forgets settings and changes numbers at random. Interactive co-creation hands out the decisions: the author picks and signs off at key points, and readers or players move the plot forward with choices and actions.

**Common designs**

1. **Candidates for each passage, the author picks**: the model offers two or more continuations at once, and the author picks one, regenerates, or undoes. arrows generates two passages each time, chosen with the arrow keys. LLooM reads the model's logits to expand several candidates at once. 1667 keeps several versions of each passage, which form a story tree. dsh-novel-craft has the author upvote or downvote candidate passages, and the model summarizes this into a preference profile of a few hundred bytes that is carried into the next round. Examples: [arrows](https://github.com/p-e-w/arrows), [LLooM](https://github.com/the-crypt-keeper/LLooM), [1667](https://github.com/1667-ai/1667), [dsh-novel-craft](https://github.com/shiyan688/dsh-novel-craft)
2. **Proposals layer by layer, the author decides**: starting from a one-line logline or a few rounds of Q&A, generate characters, beats, scenes, and prose one layer at a time. The author edits each layer before going further, and AI output counts only once it is adopted. RecurrentGPT offers several plans for the next paragraph at each step, and a person can pick one or write their own. ElyHa's proposals show up as "ghost nodes" that a person adopts. Examples: [dramatron](https://github.com/google-deepmind/dramatron), [RecurrentGPT](https://github.com/aiwaves-cn/RecurrentGPT), [wr-AI-ter](https://github.com/Think-42/wr-AI-ter), [narralume](https://github.com/abligail/narralume), [ElyHa](https://github.com/ShadowLoveElysia/ElyHa)
3. **Branches from reader choices**: after each passage, offer 2–5 choices and continue once the reader picks. interactive-fiction-skill offers five options, A–E, every turn and also accepts free input. AI-Gamble generates the whole story structure map and multiple endings at the start, then writes passage by passage according to the choices. StoryWeaver-AI pre-generates the next branches while the reader is reading. Examples: [interactive-fiction-skill](https://github.com/Treasure-hub-agent/interactive-fiction-skill), [AI-Gamble](https://github.com/xiamuceer-j/AI-Gamble), [StoryWeaver-AI](https://github.com/1195214305/StoryWeaver-AI), [cyoa](https://github.com/essencevc/cyoa)
4. **Free actions, code makes the rulings**: players act in natural language and the model only narrates; dice, numbers, and inventory are resolved by a rules engine, and any state change the model wants has to pass validation first. ai-novel-studio lets the model submit only prose and state patches, which are committed after the server validates character and scene invariants. Most common in AI tabletop RPGs and text adventures. Examples: [NeverEndingQuest](https://github.com/MoonlightByte/NeverEndingQuest), [loreweaver](https://github.com/1A7432/loreweaver), [ai-novel-studio](https://github.com/weike-zhang/ai-novel-studio), [yikes](https://github.com/psema4/yikes)
5. **Taking turns, with manual injection**: the user and the model take turns writing, and the author decides what the model sees each turn through Memory, Author's Note, and a keyword-triggered lorebook (world info). KoboldAI-Client was the first to combine the three into one system, and most roleplay frontends have kept it. The Pathweaver extension reads the recent conversation and offers up to 6 directions to choose from. Examples: [KoboldAI-Client](https://github.com/KoboldAI/KoboldAI-Client), [SillyTavern](https://github.com/SillyTavern/SillyTavern), [lite.koboldai.net](https://github.com/LostRuins/lite.koboldai.net), [SillyTavern-Pathweaver](https://github.com/mattjaybe/SillyTavern-Pathweaver)

**Trade-offs and pitfalls**

- Branches generated on the fly often never reach an ending. gpt-text-adventure lists 6 plot points at the start, and its author still notes that most stories don't get finished. The fix is to give the story an endpoint: gpt-interactive-novel sets an ending goal, and The-Endless-Tale evaluates player actions against a checklist of goals.
- If the model makes the rulings, there are no real dice. NeverEndingQuest hands dice, inventory, status, and the clock to a separate rules engine. loreweaver also notes that its deterministic parts are covered by tests, but how well the AI game master performs is a separate matter.
- A layered workflow doesn't suit everyone. The dramatron team admits that the top-down structure doesn't match every author's way of writing; playwrights in the study found the output formulaic and preferred to use it for building worlds and trying out plot changes rather than writing a full script.
- Cost and latency. Multi-agent simulation calls the model several times per turn: Sonder_Engine makes a separate call for each stage, and The-Endless-Tale takes about 40 seconds per turn. Candidates for each passage also multiply the amount generated; arrows offsets this with parallel batch generation in llama.cpp.

**Where to start**: [narralume](https://github.com/abligail/narralume) suits authors of long fiction: AI suggestions don't go into the book just because they were generated; they all start as candidates, and you can write by hand without connecting a model. [interactive-fiction-skill](https://github.com/Treasure-hub-agent/interactive-fiction-skill) suits Chinese choice-based interactive fiction: install it in an AI assistant and use it right away, with nothing to deploy. [loreweaver](https://github.com/1A7432/loreweaver) suits developers making tabletop RPGs and text adventures: rules, settings, and characters are all shareable files, and rulings are made by code.
