**What it solves**: When one model plans, writes, and checks its own work in the same context, the planning material crowds out room for writing, and it is hard for a model reviewing its own draft to find problems. Once the work is split into several roles, each call sees only the material it needs, writing and reviewing are separated, and a draft that fails review can be sent back.

**Common designs**

1. **A relay of stages**: roles are divided as in an editorial office or a writers' room. The planner, setting designer, writer, editor, and polisher each have their own prompt, and files are passed along in a fixed order. Most common in long web novels and whole-book generation; some projects also give certain roles a veto. Examples: [loom-novel](https://github.com/WadeZhao23/loom-novel), [Novel-OS](https://github.com/mrigankad/Novel-OS), [story-systems-template](https://github.com/bybren-llc/story-systems-template), [Book-Writing-AI-Agent](https://github.com/Aftabbs/Book-Writing-AI-Agent)
2. **A coordinator dispatches work based on state**: the coordinator reads the project state and decides whom to dispatch next. Each subagent has its own context, and they communicate only through artifacts written to disk. ainovel-cli writes the scheduling as deterministic code, makes a single call to the Arbiter only at the few points that need judgment, and writes a checkpoint at every step. In Claude Code-style hosts, the common shape is a main session that only assembles briefs, dispatches subagents, and writes files. Examples: [ainovel-cli](https://github.com/voocel/ainovel-cli), [Novel-factory](https://github.com/YonRen-code/Novel-factory), [awesome-novel-agent](https://github.com/modoojunko/awesome-novel-agent), [NovelGenerator](https://github.com/Dylin-code/NovelGenerator)
3. **Separate writing from review, with a review panel**: generation and review go to different agents. Several reviews can run in parallel, and an arbiter combines them; a draft that fails is sent back, with a cap on rounds. ProseForge writes four drafts in parallel and keeps the best, then three reviewers (continuity, adversarial, style) reach a joint verdict. CritiCS has several critics with personas critique the plan and the prose, and a leader combines their notes and revises. Fibb keeps only versions that are measurably better when reviewed again. Examples: [Claude-Book](https://github.com/ThomasHoussin/Claude-Book), [ProseForge](https://github.com/remacheybn408-boop/ProseForge), [CritiCS](https://github.com/EMNLP-2024-CritiCS/Collective-Critics-for-Creative-Story-Generation), [Fibb](https://github.com/Ckokoski/Fibb)
4. **Cold readers**: start a separate agent that does not inherit the author's context. It reads the finished draft in the order an ordinary reader would and reports only whether it makes sense and whether it wants to keep reading. lengdu splits author-side calibration and the reader's first read into two fresh agents, and after every revision a new Reader must recheck. novel-agent-workflow requires that the editor and the reader not have the same identity as the author. Examples: [lengdu](https://github.com/asass-11/lengdu), [novel-agent-workflow](https://github.com/realwindjpn/novel-agent-workflow), [book-genesis-studio](https://github.com/felipelobomotta-blip/book-genesis-studio)
5. **Characters act it out, a director pulls it together**: one agent per character, each acting on its own through perceive, plan, act, and reflect. Events are first played out in a sandbox, then a director or narrator organizes them into chapters or a script according to the story's goals. Sonder_Engine splits objective cause and effect, each character's perception, character decisions, and narration into separate calls, and only the commit step can write to state. Common in interactive fiction, murder mystery games, and ensemble stories. Examples: [OpenStory](https://github.com/ZJU-LLMs/OpenStory), [StoryBox](https://github.com/amcghm/StoryBox), [CoDi](https://github.com/Speeditidious/CoDi), [Sonder_Engine](https://github.com/N0819/Sonder_Engine), [fictionforge](https://github.com/wanqili857-byte/fictionforge)

**Trade-offs and pitfalls**

- **The number of calls grows with the number of roles**: Sonder_Engine calls the model several times per turn, so costs run higher. Some projects are pulling back: in v4.0, novel_generator merged 8–12 calls per chapter into 1; AI_novel cut its agents from 9 to 5; ainovel-cli states "the simpler, the more stable; no complex orchestration".
- **The same model reviewing itself**: Novel-factory puts the writer and the reviewer in two different model families and has them review candidates blind, specifically to counter self-preference. NovelGenerator runs the writer and the reviser on local models and uses Claude for the critic.
- **Rework needs a cap and an exit**: Claude-Book's three-review loop runs at most 3 rounds, StoryClaw sends a draft back at most 3 times, novel_generator uses a circuit breaker to prevent endless rewriting, and NovelGenerator logs chapters that still haven't converged after two rounds in issues.md.
- **Information leaks break the division of labor**: lengdu does not allow the author's intent or the calibration report to be revealed to the Reader. hermes-novel-generator has character agents plan scenes without seeing each other. Novel-factory strips meta-information from the writer's view and scans the text verbatim for keywords from the mystery's solution.
- **Free performance doesn't mean there is a story**: StoryBox and CoDi both add top-down planning or a director on top of character interaction. Interactive settings also have to account for latency: in openovel the foreground narrator only streams out prose, while background agents such as the director and memory update the state files asynchronously.

**Where to start**:

- [ainovel-cli](https://github.com/voocel/ainovel-cli): suits developers who want to build their own pipeline. The README clearly explains its three-layer division of work (a deterministic engine, autonomous workers, and arbitration on demand). You can watch it write in the TUI, or run it headless.
- [Claude-Book](https://github.com/ThomasHoussin/Claude-Book): suits people writing books with Claude Code: the bible is read-only, state is versioned per chapter, the three reviews have a round limit, and the directory structure is simple and easy to change.
- [lengdu](https://github.com/asass-11/lengdu): suits people who already have a finished draft and only want to add an independent reader check. The workflow is short; its core is one SKILL.md and a few role profiles.
